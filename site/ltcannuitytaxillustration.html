Why the difference? Under the Pension Protection Act (IRC §7702B(e) & §72(e)(11)), distributions from a PPA-compliant annuity used for qualified long-term care are received income-tax-free — the otherwise-taxable gain comes out without tax. A standard distribution is taxed gains-first (LIFO) at your ordinary-income rate, so the early years carry the heaviest tax until the gain is exhausted.

Important: This tool is an educational illustration, not tax, legal, or investment advice. It assumes a non-qualified annuity (funded with after-tax dollars) purchased after August 13, 1982, where standard partial distributions are taxed gains-first (LIFO) under IRC §72. The year-by-year schedule assumes a level annual distribution with no additional growth on the remaining balance, and runs until the value is depleted (capped at 30 years). Tax-free treatment of long-term care distributions requires a Pension Protection Act–compliant annuity with a qualified long-term care feature, and applies only to distributions used for qualified long-term care services as defined under IRC §7702B; per-diem (indemnity) benefits may be subject to IRS daily limits. Qualified LTC distributions reduce the contract's cost basis. Tax-qualified (IRA/403(b)) annuities are taxed differently and are not modeled here. Marginal tax rate is applied as a simplification and does not account for bracket layering, IRMAA, Social Security taxation, or state-specific rules. Figures are estimates — actual results depend on the specific contract, carrier, and your circumstances. Consult a qualified tax advisor and licensed insurance professional before acting.
